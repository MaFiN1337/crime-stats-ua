

##  Technical Report: Analysis of Factors Influencing Crimes under Article 185 of the Criminal Code (Theft), 2014-2024

This report analyzes the dynamics of crimes registered under **Article 185 of the Criminal Code of Ukraine (Theft)** for the period **2014-2024** and tests the proposed hypotheses based on economic theories of crime (specifically, Gary Becker's theory) and empirical models.

---

###  Dynamics of Registered Crimes (Article 185)

The overall dynamics of registered crimes under Article 185 (monthly) show significant fluctuations between 2014 and 2024, but the general trend, especially since 2016, is one of **decline** in the number of registered cases.

* **2014-2016:** Sharp increase and peak in registered crimes in 2016 (over 35k cases per month).
* **2017-2021:** Gradual but steady decrease in the number of thefts.
* **2022:** A sharp drop in registered crimes. This decline is likely associated with the start of the full-scale war, which caused:
    * **Mass population migration** (especially from occupied and frontline territories).
    * **A shift in law enforcement priorities** and difficulties with registration in parts of the territory.
    * **An effective decrease in the population** in the government-controlled territory.
* **2023-2024:** The number of registered crimes stabilizes at a relatively low level (an average of about 5-7k cases per month until August 2024).

###  Hypothesis Testing and Factor Analysis

The proposed hypotheses are based on the premise that economic hardship (unemployment, inflation) increases the material motivation for committing crimes (theft).

#### 1. Hypothesis: Unemployment → Increase in Theft

**Mechanism:** A decrease in the "opportunity cost" of legal work due to unemployment makes illegal means of obtaining income (theft) more attractive.

| Year | Unemployment Dynamics (Registered) | Theft Dynamics (Article 185) | Trend Correlation |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **2014-2016** | Relatively stable, then a slight decrease. | Sharp **increase** to a peak. | **Refuted** (Unemployment fell in 2016-2017 while thefts peaked) |
| **2017-2021** | Gradual decrease. | Gradual **decrease**. | **Confirmed** (Theft trend follows the unemployment trend, but correlation is weak) |
| **2022-2024** | Sharp drop (due to methodological change/war). | Sharp **drop** (military factors). | **Not Representative** (Military factors dominate) |

**Conclusion:** In the initial period (2014-2016), the correlation is not confirmed (thefts grew faster than the change in unemployment). A trend alignment towards a decrease is observed in 2017-2021. Since 2022, the dominant impact of the war makes economic indicators less significant in explaining the dynamics of registered crimes.

---

#### 2. Hypothesis: Inflation / Rising Prices → Increase in Petty Theft

**Mechanism:** Rising prices for food and essential goods create a sense of scarcity/hardship, which increases the likelihood of petty thefts (e.g., shoplifting).

| Year | Inflation Dynamics (%) | Theft Dynamics (Article 185) | Trend Correlation |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **2014-2015** | Sharp **increase** in inflation (up to 40%). | Sharp **increase** in theft. | **Confirmed** |
| **2016-2021** | Decrease in inflation to low rates. | Steady **decrease** in theft. | **Confirmed** |
| **2022-2023** | Sharp **increase** in inflation (2022 peak). | Sharp **drop** in theft (military factors). | **Refuted** |

**Conclusion:** A strong correlation was observed before 2022: **when inflation rose, thefts also rose, and vice versa.** This confirms that the perception of economic hardship and financial pressure (inflation) is a powerful factor motivating theft. However, in 2022-2023, despite high inflation, the number of registered thefts dropped sharply, indicating the **dominance of non-economic factors (war, migration)**.

---

### Additional Factor: Decriminalization

The analysis of registered crime dynamics in 2024 shows a significant change starting in **August (Month 8)** and the following months (9-12), where the number of registered crimes **drops sharply** (to 2-3k cases/month).

* **Fact:** **In August 2024**, the law on **decriminalization of theft up to 3,028 UAH** (Part 1 of Art. 185) came into effect.
* **Impact on Data:** The sharp drop in registered crimes in August-December 2024 **does not mean that the actual number of thefts decreased.** It means that a significant portion of petty thefts, which were previously registered under Article 185 of the Criminal Code, are now **no longer criminal offenses** and are likely being registered under administrative articles.

**Conclusion:** Decriminalization is a **statistical factor** that influenced the **registered number of criminal offenses** but cannot be interpreted as a reduction in actual criminal activity.

---

### Overall Summary

| Hypothesis | Analysis and Evidence | Confirmation/Refutation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Unemployment → Increase in Theft** | Weak trend alignment before 2021; military factors dominate since 2022. | **Not Fully Confirmed** |
| **2. Inflation → Increase in Theft** | Strong correlation before 2022: rising inflation coincided with rising thefts. | **Not Fully Confirmed**  |

**Key Findings of the Report:**

1.  **Economic factors (Inflation)** were indeed important drivers of theft dynamics until 2022, thereby **confirming** economic theories of crime.
2.  Since **2022**, the dynamics of registered crimes **are predominantly determined by non-economic factors**, such as **war, migration, and population decrease.** These factors outweigh the influence of economic hardship (unemployment, high inflation).
3.  **Since August 2024**, the dynamics of registered crimes under Article 185 have seen a sharp decline due to **decriminalization**, which is a **change in registration** and not a true reduction in the crime rate.
